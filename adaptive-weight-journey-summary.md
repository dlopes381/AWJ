# Adaptive Weight Journey — App Summary

A single-file HTML/CSS/JS web app (no build step, no backend) that turns a weight goal into a personalized, self-adjusting calorie roadmap. Supports loss, gain, and maintenance. Mobile-first, ~480px max width, bottom tab navigation.

## Core concept
User enters body stats (weight, height, age, sex, activity level) and a goal. The app estimates real energy needs via BMR/TDEE, then breaks the weight range into "phases" at a fixed interval (e.g. every 5 lbs). Each phase has its own maintenance and eating calorie target. As the user's *rolling average* weight crosses a phase's target, the calorie target automatically steps to the next phase. Over time, the app also compares actual logged results against the formula estimate and self-corrects.

## Calorie engine
- **BMR**: Mifflin-St Jeor formula (`calcBMR`) — `10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age (+5 male / −161 female)`.
- **TDEE**: `calcTDEE` = BMR × activity multiplier (sedentary 1.20 / light 1.375 / moderate 1.55 / veryActive 1.725).
- **Phase calories are recalculated fresh at each phase's target weight** (`tdeeAtWeight`), not scaled proportionally from the starting TDEE — proportional scaling was a bug (it scaled the whole TDEE including the weight-independent BMR terms, understating calories at lower weights by 300+ cal in a typical 90 lb journey). Fixed.
- **Goal types**: loss (`maintenance − deficit`), gain (`maintenance + surplus`), maintain (`= maintenance`).
- **Legacy fallback**: profiles without body stats (`height`/`age`/`sex`/`activityLevel`) fall back to `weightTarget × multiplier`, preserving pre-upgrade behavior.
- **Manual override**: `calcMode: 'multiplier'` lets advanced users force the flat multiplier formula even with body stats present.

## Metabolic calibration (`computeCalibration`)
Compares actual logged calories eaten vs. actual weight trend over a 28-day window (needs ≥10 food logs and ≥4 weigh-ins in that window, **and those weigh-ins must span at least `CALIBRATION_MIN_SPAN_DAYS` = 21 actual days**) to derive the user's *real* TDEE via energy balance (`actualTDEE = avgCaloriesEaten − weightChangeSlope×3500`). The ratio of actual-to-formula TDEE becomes a calibration factor (clamped to ±25%) applied multiplicatively to all future phase calorie calculations. The 21-day span requirement (matching `MIN_GOAL_TRACKING_DAYS` used by the completion estimate) was added after a user with only 15 days of gappy logging — but already past the 10-log/4-weigh-in count thresholds — got a confident-looking -13% calibration that dragged a 300+ lb user's calorie target down to 1,883 cal, an aggressive number for that body weight built on a short, noisy stretch rather than a real trend. Purely derived live from logs each render — nothing persisted, so it's never stale. `computeCalibration()` always returns a diagnostic object (`{active, nutritionLogsTracked, nutritionLogsNeeded, daysTracked, daysNeeded, ...}`) rather than a bare `null` when inactive, so the dashboard's Current-strategy note, the Roadmap banner, and the Calc Details page can explain *why* calibration hasn't kicked in yet (e.g. "needs 21 days of tracked history — 15 so far") instead of silently doing nothing. Surfaced on the dashboard, the calc-details page, and the Roadmap tab.

## Deficit tapering (optional, `profile.taperDeficit`)
Gradually eases the deficit/surplus by up to 40% over the final third of the journey (by weight distance, not time), matching common coaching guidance to protect muscle as body fat gets lower. Off by default.

## Rolling average gating
Weight fluctuates day to day, so phase advancement is NOT driven by the single latest scale reading:
- `rollingAverageWeight(n=5)`: averages the last 5 logged weigh-ins.
- All phase-progression logic — active phase, "goal reached," calorie target, roadmap badges — is direction-aware (`phaseDirection`) and driven off this rolling average, so it works symmetrically for loss (descending phases) and gain (ascending phases).
- The raw latest weight is still shown separately on the dashboard ("Current weight") alongside the rolling average.

## Completion estimate (`estimateCompletion`)
Projects a recent-window (21-day, min 4 points) linear regression forward to estimate a "next phase" date and a "goal weight" date. The near-term phase estimate shows readily; the far-out goal-date estimate is gated by two checks — minimum 21 days of tracking history, and the projection can't exceed 6× the days actually tracked — to avoid a noisy 2-week trend producing a falsely precise date 6+ months out. Shows "insufficient data" or "stalled" (wrong-direction trend) fallback messages otherwise.

## Weight trend chart (`renderTrendChart`) and its minimum-history guard
Runs a full-history linear regression (`computeTrend`/`leastSquaresSlope`) over every weigh-in and reports a "lbs/week" pace with a colored arrow (losing fast / trending down / flat / trending up). Guarded by `MIN_TREND_SPAN_DAYS = 4`: if the first and most recent weigh-in span fewer than 4 days, the slope is too noisy to extrapolate into a believable weekly rate (e.g. 3 weigh-ins one day apart, each 1.5–2 lbs apart, regress out to a "13 lbs/week" claim that's really just normal day-to-day fluctuation) — the label falls back to "Only N days of history so far — keep logging for a reliable weekly trend" instead of a specific rate, matching the same kind of safeguard already used by `estimateCompletion`. The chart itself (raw points, trend line) still renders regardless.

## Weekly insight (`renderWeeklyInsight`) and its reconciliation note
Compares the average of the last 7 logged weigh-ins against the prior 7 (simple week-over-week delta, not a regression), giving pace-specific advice ("faster than ideal, consider adding calories" / "right in the ideal range" / "progress has slowed" / "hasn't moved much"). Because it's just two small raw-average windows, a single accepted outlier weigh-in inside either window can swing it independently of the full-history `computeTrend()` regression shown in "Weight trend" below — confirmed during a 30-day sanity-check simulation where an accepted 15 lb one-day spike produced "averaging 3.5 lbs/week, faster than ideal" right next to "Roughly flat this stretch" for the same data. Both numbers were individually correct per their own method, but contradicted each other with no explanation. Fixed by comparing the two rates (`rate` vs. `-trend.slopePerWeek`, same sign convention) and appending a short note — "Your longer-term Weight trend below tells a different story — a single unusual weigh-in in the last two weeks can skew this short-term number" — whenever they diverge by more than 2 lbs/week, rather than reconciling the underlying statistics (each window serves a genuinely different purpose: short-term pace vs. long-term trend).

## Rolling average charts (7-day / 14-day)
Two additional dashboard charts — "Calorie intake trend" (below Calorie breakdown) and "Rolling weight average" (below Weight trend) — smooth out day-to-day noise by averaging over a trailing *calendar-day* window at each logged date, via the shared `rollingWindowSeries(sortedLogs, dateKey, valueFn, windowDays)` helper. This differs from `rollingAverageWeight()`, which averages the last N *entries* regardless of date spacing — the rolling-chart windows are strictly calendar-day-based (7 and 14 days), so they stay meaningful even with logging gaps.
- `renderRollingLineChart(logs, dateKey, valueFn, opts)` is a generic renderer shared by both charts. The 14-day average is the primary/headline series (thicker solid pine line, dots, listed first in the legend and tooltip) since it's less noisy day-to-day; the 7-day average is secondary context (thinner dashed gold line, drawn underneath).
- `renderRollingWeightChart()` feeds it `state.weightLogs` / weight; `renderRollingCalorieChart()` feeds it `state.nutritionLogs` / `nutritionTotalCalories(n)`.

## Rolling weight insight (`renderRollingWeightInsight`, below the Rolling weight average chart)
Gives goal/phase-aware coaching tips based on the 7-day vs. 14-day rolling averages themselves — distinct from `renderWeeklyInsight`'s raw last-7-vs-prior-7 comparison, and requires ≥7 weight logs. Since the 14-day window fully contains the 7-day one, `(14-day avg − 7-day avg) × 2` approximates an "implied lbs/week" rate under a roughly linear trend (half the last week's change shows up in that gap); the sign is flipped for a `gain` goal (where rising is the desired direction) so the rate is always positive when moving toward the goal. Message tiers by goal type:
- **maintain**: within ±1 lb/week of flat → "holding steady"; otherwise names the drift direction and suggests a small calorie nudge the opposite way.
- **loss/gain**: negative implied rate (moving the wrong way) → suggests double-checking calorie adherence; >3 lbs/week → suggests easing up (add calories for loss, trim back for gain) since that's ahead of a sustainable 1–2 lb/week pace; 1–3 → encouraging "nice momentum"; under 1 → "progress has slowed."
- For loss/gain (not maintain), if the goal isn't reached yet, appends the distance to the next phase's calorie step and — reusing `estimateCompletion()` rather than deriving a second, potentially-conflicting estimate — an ETA only when that function's status is `'ok'` with a confident `phaseDate` (omitted entirely for a stalled/wrong-direction trend, same guard the dashboard's own timeline card uses).
- O(n²) (each point's window is recomputed by filtering the full sorted array) — acceptable given personal-tracking data volumes; not optimized further.

## Weight trend & forecast (`WeightTrendEngine`)
A dedicated calculation layer (a plain object, not a separate file) that turns noisy day-to-day weigh-ins into a calm, honest story of progress, separate from the calorie/calibration engine above: **the trend system observes ("what's actually happening"), the adaptive engine decides ("what to do about it")** — nothing below writes to `state` or feeds back into `computePhases`/`computeCalibration`. Reuses `leastSquaresSlope`/`computeTrend`/`computeRecentTrend` (both now accept an optional `logs` argument, defaulting to `state.weightLogs`, so every pre-existing call site is unaffected) rather than re-implementing windowed regression.

**Two different window-selection policies answer two different questions — this distinction is load-bearing and the names stay verbose in code on purpose:**
- **`currentTrend`** ("what's happening recently?" — the dashboard headline, and Progress Check's "actual" rate) prefers the **14-day** window, falling back to 7-day then 30-day. 14-day is the deliberate balance point between 7-day noise and 30-day staleness: hand-testing against `30d=-0.2 / 14d=-1.8 / 7d=-2.0` (a real recent acceleration after an earlier stall) and `30d=-2.0 / 14d=-0.4 / 7d=+1.5` (a real recent stall/reversal) showed that always preferring the longest available window fails exactly when it matters — it either badly understates a genuine recent acceleration or falsely reassures through a genuine recent stall. Preferring 14-day reports both cases correctly.
- **`forecastBasisWindow`** ("what rate is safe to extrapolate months out?") prefers the **30-day** window, falling back to 14-day then 7-day, then gets clamped against the full-history `overall` trend (below) before becoming `forecastRatePerWeek`.
- **`overall`** is the full-history regression, and is intentionally **all-time — not scoped to the current profile/goal** (same v1 boundary `estimateCompletion()`'s `trackedDays` gate already accepts). A user who changed goals or regimes mid-history can see this produce a `'conflicting'` forecast rather than a falsely precise date — the safe failure mode. This limitation belongs to `forecastBasisWindow`'s clamp specifically, not to `currentTrend`, which the 14-day-preferred policy already answers cleanly on its own.

**Conservative forecast clamp**: `forecastRatePerWeek` is a sign-matched `min(|forecastBasisWindow|, |overall|)` — precisely, it always picks whichever of the two is **slower**, in either direction, so a short hot streak can never overpromise and a stale historical average can never paper over a genuine recent slowdown. Worked example matching the app's own validation dataset (7-day≈-1.9, 14-day≈-5.0, 30-day≈-3.5, overall≈-2.5 lb/week): `forecastBasisWindow` picks the 30-day window (-3.5, available and preferred over 14/7), clamped against `overall` (-2.5) → **forecast lands at -2.5 lb/week**, never touching the -5.0 outlier. If the two disagree on direction entirely (`Math.sign` mismatch) the rate is `null` with `forecastStatus:'conflicting'` — distinct from `'insufficient'` (not enough data at all), since they need different copy ("your trend looks like it's shifting" vs. "keep logging a little longer"). The conflict check compares `overall` against **both** `forecastBasisWindow` **and** `currentTrend`, not just the former — found by testing a synthetic "gaining for a long stretch, then losing for the last 10 days" dataset: `forecastBasisWindow` (30-day) can still be mostly the old regime and so agree with `overall` by pure coincidence, while `currentTrend` (14-day) has already caught the reversal. Checking both closes that gap.

**Outlier leverage is real and position-dependent** (measured by hand, not assumed): regressing a flat-ish week `[293,294,293,295,294,292,293]` gives -0.75 lb/wk; changing the *last* point to 302 (one bad reading) flips the slope to **+6.0 lb/wk**; the same +9 spike placed at the *middle* of the same window barely moves it (still ≈-0.75), since a point at the window's mean x has ~zero leverage on slope by construction. Per design, the regression itself is **not** made "robust" and outliers are never modified or hidden — actual measurements stay visible everywhere. Mitigated instead by: (1) `currentTrend` preferring 14-day over 7-day already roughly halves worst-case single-point leverage; (2) a `divergenceNote` (same reconciliation-note pattern already shipped in `renderWeeklyInsight`) fires when the raw 7-day rate differs from the chosen `currentTrend` by more than 2 lb/week, turning outlier sensitivity into the transparency signal the feature is meant to teach rather than hiding it.

**Confidence** (`trendConfidence`) reuses existing thresholds rather than a new statistical-quality system: `'insufficient'` below `MIN_COMPLETION_POINTS` (4) total weigh-ins or a full-history span under `MIN_TREND_SPAN_DAYS` (4 days); `'established'` once that span reaches `MIN_GOAL_TRACKING_DAYS` (21 days); `'emerging'` in between. A dataset that's technically large enough but noisy/contradictory is already caught by the `'conflicting'` forecast status above, so no separate regression-quality (R²) system was added on top.

**Goal date** (`forecastGoal`) mirrors `estimateCompletion()`'s own two-part guard exactly, via a shared `isExtrapolationConfident(trackedDays, projectedDays)` helper (`trackedDays >= MIN_GOAL_TRACKING_DAYS && projectedDays <= trackedDays * MAX_GOAL_EXTRAPOLATION_RATIO`) rather than a second copy of the same expression — used by both functions now. Skips straight to `'stalled'` when the (already-conservative) forecast rate doesn't point toward the goal (via `getGoalProgressDirection`, below). This makes the **Goal forecast** card the sole "when will I reach my goal" figure app-wide: `renderEstimatedTimeline()`'s "Goal weight" column (a single 21-day-window extrapolation that could silently disagree with this more conservative multi-window one) was removed in favor of it — confirmed safe since its only other caller, `renderRollingWeightInsight()`, never read those fields.

**Progress check** (`compareExpectedVsActual`) compares the calorie plan's implied rate (`taperedAdjustment(p, active.weightTarget) / CAL_PER_LB * 7`, always a positive magnitude) against `currentTrend`, both normalized so positive means "toward the goal" (see `normalizeToGoalProgress` below). Classification is **ratio-based**, not a flat additive tolerance: `abs(actual − plan) <= max(abs(plan) * 0.35, 0.3)` → `'on-pace'`. An earlier `max(0.5, 25%·plan)` version was tested against `plan=-0.5 / actual=-1.0` (losing exactly double the planned rate) and wrongly called it on-pace because the flat 0.5 lb/wk floor dominated for small plan rates — the ratio-based version fixes that case correctly. Near-zero actual (`< TREND_NOISE_EPSILON*7` lb/week) is `'plateaued'`; actual pointing the wrong way is `'reversing'` — checked before the tolerance math, since those are qualitatively different from "just slower," not merely a smaller number. **Purely informational**: never suggests or triggers a calorie/calibration change on its own, and respects the existing calibration safeguards untouched — excluded entirely for `maintain` goals (a "planned rate" would be fabricated, since `taperedAdjustment` returns a deficit value that's structurally unused for maintain) and once the goal is already reached.

**Goal-aware progress direction** — one canonical, shared definition used by all of the above *and* by the pre-existing `renderTrendChart()`:
```js
normalizeToGoalProgress(ratePerWeek, goalType) // positive = toward goal, negative = away, 0 = maintain
getGoalProgressDirection(ratePerWeek, goalType) // 'toward' | 'away' | 'neutral'
```
Fixed a real pre-existing bug while touching this chart for the goal-line/projection work below: `renderTrendChart()`'s color/label logic always treated a negative slope as good (pine/gold) and positive as bad (danger red), regardless of goal type — backwards for a `gain`-goal user successfully gaining weight. Now buckets on `Math.abs(slopePerWeek)` (same 2.5/0.75→0.25 breakpoints as before, applied symmetrically) plus `getGoalProgressDirection` for color; the arrow glyph and "Trending up/down" wording stay tied to the *literal* weight direction (still accurate regardless of goal) — only the color/tier language ("fast", pine vs. danger) is now goal-aware.

**Chart enhancements** (`renderTrendChart`, same function as above): a dashed goal-weight reference line, drawn only when the goal falls within 1.5× the plotted weight range of the current readings (otherwise silently omitted, so a goal far outside current readings can't flatten the whole chart into an unreadable line); and a dashed forward projection from the last point toward the goal, drawn only when `forecastGoal()` itself (the same single source of truth as the Goal forecast card) is confident, with an explicit **"(projected)"** text label so it can't be mistaken for a real measurement. The chart's x-axis is index-spaced, not a literal date scale, so the projection's x-position is approximated from the forecast's day-distance using the plotted points' average day-spacing.

**Empty/sparse-data states**: `n===0` weigh-ins → "Start logging your weight to see your trend."; `1 <= n < MIN_COMPLETION_POINTS` → "Keep logging your weight to establish a trend."; enough points but `confidence==='insufficient'` (too short a span) → "Your trend will become more reliable after you've logged over a longer period." (matches the spec's required three-tier empty-state copy exactly). The Goal forecast and Progress check cards simply don't render at all below `MIN_COMPLETION_POINTS` — no partial/misleading forecast is ever shown.

**Staleness**: `computeRecentTrend`'s windows are anchored to the *last logged date*, not wall-clock today (same idiom as `rollingWindowSeries` elsewhere), so a stale last weigh-in produces a stale-but-otherwise-normal window rather than a crash. To avoid silently calling a stale window "current," the trend strip appends `(as of <date>)` whenever the last log isn't today or yesterday — one honest label rather than a new staleness-tier system.

## Milestone-based journey (`state.milestones`, `recordGoalMilestone`)
Extending a goal (e.g. 280 → 220 after reaching 280) no longer erases the fact the earlier goal was reached. `state.milestones` is a new, additive, backward-compatible array of `{weight, reachedDate}`, defaulted alongside `weightLogs`/`nutritionLogs` everywhere the app constructs a fresh state shape (initial `state`, `loadState()`'s defaulting block, `importDataFile()`'s reconstructed object — which hand-picks fields rather than spreading the parsed JSON, so this needed adding explicitly — and the "Reset all data" handler).

`recordGoalMilestone(oldProfile, newGoalWeight)` runs in the Settings save handler immediately before `state.profile` is overwritten (so `isGoalReached()`, which reads `state.profile` directly, still evaluates the *old* goal). The only thing that matters is **whether the old goal was reached, period** — the new goal's direction is irrelevant. An earlier version gated on "is the new goal more ambitious than the old one," which was actually a bug: someone who reached 220 and then raised their goal back to 250 would silently lose credit for 220 under that rule. A dedup check (`state.milestones.some(m=>m.weight===oldProfile.goalWeight)`) prevents duplicate entries if a goal value is revisited (e.g. 280→220→280→220). Excluded for `maintain` goals (nothing meaningful to preserve, since `goalWeight` always equals `startWeight` there) and for a no-op settings save where the goal field wasn't touched.

`reachedDate` is realistically the date the goal was *changed*, not necessarily the exact date it was first crossed (there's no historical point-in-time rolling-average calculation to derive that precisely, and it wasn't judged worth building) — documented here as a known simplification, and deliberately **not shown** in the breadcrumb UI, so it never implies false precision about *when*.

`renderMilestoneBreadcrumb()` (Roadmap tab) renders start → past milestone(s) → "you are here" (current rolling average) → current goal as a short vertical list — coarser than, and not a duplicate of, the existing fine-grained phase list below it.

## Engagement features (streaks, quick log, undo, celebrations, PWA)
- **Logging streak** (`computeLoggingStreak`): counts consecutive *calendar* days with either a weight or nutrition entry, ending today. If today hasn't been logged yet, counting starts from yesterday so the streak doesn't drop to 0 first thing each morning. Shown on the dashboard next to a **7-day consistency stat** (`loggingConsistency(7)` — how many of the last 7 calendar days have any entry).
- **Quick log card** (`quickLogEntry`, dashboard-only): a single "Today, `<date>`" card with a weight field and a calories field, always scoped to today's date (no date picker — that's what "+ Log full activity" is for). Behavior branches on whether today is already logged (`todaysLogged`):
  - **Not yet logged**: both fields are editable, button reads "Log today's entry"; submitting calls `quickLogEntry(weightVal, calVal)` — weight replaces today's weigh-in (same outlier guard and phase/goal-crossing detection as the full save flow), and a calorie value creates a single "Other"-labeled meal line item.
  - **Already logged**: the fields become `readonly` (dimmed background, today's actual saved values) and the button reads "Edit today's entry" — clicking it does *not* re-save anything; it calls `goTab('log')` + `startEditActivity(todayStr())` to jump straight into the Log tab's real edit row for today (itemized meals, protein/carbs/fat/water, notes), since blindly resubmitting two bare fields over an existing itemized day was error-prone and confusing. `quickLogEntry`'s own guard against flattening itemized meals (only touches nutrition if the value differs from the current total) still exists for the not-yet-logged path where it can still fire on a resubmit.
- **Milestone/goal celebrations**: both the quick-log path and the full activity-form save now check for a goal-reached transition (`isGoalReached()` before/after) in addition to the existing phase-crossing check, and surface a distinct 🎉 toast for reaching the goal itself vs. a 🏁 toast for a phase-calorie step change.
- **Undo-instead-of-confirm delete** (`deleteActivity`): per-day delete on the Log tab no longer blocks on a `confirm()` dialog — it deletes immediately and shows a toast with a clickable "Undo" action (5s) that restores the exact removed weight/nutrition entries. `showToast(msg, opts)` was extended with `opts.actionLabel`/`opts.onAction`/`opts.duration` to support this; existing plain-message calls are unaffected. (The unrelated "Reset all data" and "Import data" confirms, and the weigh-in outlier-jump confirms, are untouched — those guard irreversible bulk operations or data-entry typos, not routine single-day corrections.)
- **Chart tooltips**: the weight-trend chart and both rolling-average charts now have the same hover-tooltip behavior as the Trail SVG (oversized invisible hit-circles + a floating tooltip div). `showTrailTip`/`hideTrailTip` were generalized to take an optional `tipId` (defaulting to `'trail-tip'` for backward compatibility) so multiple charts on the same page can each have their own tooltip element without colliding.
- **PWA-lite polish**: an inline SVG data-URI favicon/apple-touch-icon (reusing the header's mountain-path mark) and a `<link rel="manifest">` (also an inline data URI — no extra files, staying single-file) with `display: standalone` plus `theme-color`/`apple-mobile-web-app-*` meta tags, so "Add to Home Screen" gets an app name, tinted browser chrome, and a standalone window. No service worker/offline support — that would need real hosting and was out of scope for a single-file app.
- **Collapsed advanced nutrition fields**: Protein/Carbs/Fat/Water in both the new-entry form and the edit form are tucked behind a native `<details>`/`<summary>` disclosure ("Protein, carbs, fat & water (optional)"), collapsed by default to keep the common case (weight + calories) short. The edit form's disclosure auto-opens (`open` attribute) if that day already has any of those values set, so existing data is visible without an extra tap.
- **Collapsed new-entry form** (Log tab): the whole Date/Weight/Calories/Notes/Save-entry card is now itself wrapped in a top-level `<details>` ("+ Add or back-date an entry"), collapsed by default — since same-day weight/calories are normally handled by the dashboard's quick-log card, this form is mainly for back-dating a missed day or adding itemized detail. Landing on the Log tab via the bottom nav shows just the collapsed toggle + History; the dashboard's "+ Log full activity" button sets a one-shot `openLogEntryForm` flag before navigating so the form opens pre-expanded when the user explicitly asked for it, then the flag resets so later plain visits to the Log tab stay collapsed.
- **Redesigned History cards** (`renderActivityRow`, Log tab): restructured from a single stacked text block into a clearer layout — a header row (date + edit/delete icons), a stat row with labeled weight/calorie values (reusing the dashboard's caption/value visual language), and a divider-separated line for macro/notes text when present. `.del-btn` also got a hover background for better affordance.
- **Dashboard section order**: weight-focused charts (Weight trend, Rolling weight average) now come before calorie-focused charts (Calorie breakdown, Calorie intake trend), and the Weekly insight banner moved from the very bottom of the page to right after the hero card/banners, so it's visible without scrolling.

## Data model (stored client-side only)
```js
state = {
  profile: {
    name, startWeight, goalWeight, height, age, sex, activityLevel, goalType,
    multiplier, recommendedMultiplier, bmr, tdee,
    calcMode, deficit, interval, taperDeficit, createdDate
  },
  weightLogs: [{ id, date, weight, notes }],
  nutritionLogs: [{ id, date, meals: [{ id, label, calories }], protein, carbs, fat, water }],
  milestones: [{ weight, reachedDate }]
}
```
- `weightLogs` and `nutritionLogs` remain separate arrays (one entry per date per type), but the UI presents them merged as a single daily "activity" — see Log tab below. They're joined purely by matching `date` strings; there's no shared id between a day's weight entry and its nutrition entry.
- **Calories are itemized, not a single number**: each `nutritionLogs` entry holds a `meals` array, each item with its own `calories` and a `label` constrained to a fixed set: `MEAL_CATEGORIES = ['Breakfast','Lunch','Dinner','Snack','Drinks','Other']` (picked via dropdown, not free text — needed for the category chart to have stable, groupable buckets). The displayed/used total is always `nutritionTotalCalories(n)` — the live sum of `meals[].calories` — never a stored total, so it can't drift out of sync.
- **Legacy migration**: older entries saved before the itemized-meals upgrade had a flat `calories` number and no `meals` array; entries from before the fixed-category dropdown had free-text (often blank) labels. `migrateNutritionLogs()` runs on every load (and on import) and backfills both: missing `meals` arrays become a single `Other`-labeled item, and any label not in `MEAL_CATEGORIES` is coerced to `Other`. So old data displays and sums (and buckets into the donut chart) correctly with no user action.
- Persisted via `localStorage` (key `awj:data`) — local to the browser/origin, no server, no auth, no cross-device sync.
- **Export/Import**: Settings has an "Export data" button (downloads a timestamped JSON file, itemized meals included as-is) and "Import data" (file picker, validates shape, runs the same legacy-meals migration, confirms before overwriting) — the only backup/portability mechanism. Verified round-trip-safe for both current-format and pre-upgrade legacy exports.
- One entry per date per log type (saving on a date that already has an entry overwrites it).
- **Outlier guard**: saving a weigh-in that's an implausible jump from the last entry (threshold scales with days-since-last-log, so real long-gap changes aren't flagged) prompts a confirmation before saving — protects the rolling average, calibration, and completion-estimate math from typos. Applies the same way whether the weight was entered via the unified activity form or its inline edit row.

## Screens (bottom tab bar)
1. **Trail (Dashboard)** — reworked so the first viewport answers "what do I weigh / eat today / how close am I / what should I log" without scrolling (see "Dashboard hierarchy pass" below): hero card (current weight, goal, today's calorie target — now the visually dominant number — calories eaten/remaining, progress bar); a **milestone card** (next-phase target, distance remaining, its own progress bar, distinct "reached" styling); a compact, cardless **trend strip** (`renderTrendStrip`, see "Weight trend & forecast" below) right after it, answering "how fast am I currently losing" and "when might I reach my goal" without adding a second full card to this always-visible tier; low-calorie-floor banner when applicable; stat grid (total lost/gained + rolling avg, current phase + "Phase X of Y", current phase calories, estimated maintenance); a "Quick log" block — streak/consistency stat card plus the one-tap weight-and-calories entry card scoped to today (read-only + "Edit today's entry" once already logged, see Engagement features below) — and the "+ Log full activity" button. Everything below that (Weekly insight, Current strategy, Estimated timeline, **Weight trend & forecast / Goal forecast / Progress check**, SVG trail visualization, Weight trend, Rolling weight average + its coaching banner, Calorie breakdown, Calorie intake trend) lives inside a collapsed-by-default **Insights** disclosure. Within Insights, **Rolling weight average** is now listed and visually emphasized ahead of the daily **Weight trend** chart (whose raw per-day line/dots are rendered lighter/thinner — secondary context to the trend line), matching the fact that the rolling average is the more meaningful day-to-day signal; **Current strategy** is itself a nested, collapsed disclosure (summary shows just the deficit/surplus inline) rather than an always-visible card, since most users don't need to see it daily.
2. **Roadmap** — full phase list with Reached/Current/Upcoming badges and each phase's calorie targets, plus a banner disclosing when calibration/taper are affecting the numbers shown. Visual weight is no longer uniform: completed phases are faded (`phase-completed`), the current phase gets a heavier border, elevation, larger type, and a solid (not tinted) "Current" badge (`phase-current`), and upcoming phases stay neutral — so the active step is identifiable at a glance without reading badge text. A **Journey milestones** breadcrumb (`renderMilestoneBreadcrumb`, see "Milestone-based journey" below) sits above the phase list once the user has at least one recorded milestone — a coarse start → past-goals → "you are here" → current-goal view, distinct from the fine-grained phase list below it.
3. **Log** — unified "Log activity" tab (formerly separate Weight/Calories sub-tabs, now merged): a collapsed "+ Add or back-date an entry" disclosure (auto-expands only when reached via the dashboard's "+ Log full activity" button — see Engagement features) containing one form per day with Date, Weight, and a Calories section. Above the itemized rows, a row of quick-add chips (Breakfast/Lunch/Dinner/Snack/Drinks/Other — `quickAddMeal()`) appends a pre-labeled, auto-focused calorie line item in one tap (reusing the still-blank default row instead of stacking an empty one), so the common case doesn't require opening the category dropdown; the dropdown remains for re-labeling or less-common categories. "+ Add another" still appends a blank row directly. A further-collapsed "Protein, carbs, fat & water (optional)" sub-disclosure (auto-opens when editing a day that already has any of those set) and Notes follow. History lists one combined row per day; **today's row is visually highlighted** (tinted background/border + a "Today" pill) so it's identifiable while scrolling past prior entries, each with Edit (reopens that day's weight and full itemized calorie list for editing) and Delete (deletes immediately with an "Undo" toast rather than a confirmation prompt, see Engagement features above).
4. **Setup/Settings** — collects body stats, goal type, activity level; live BMR/TDEE/multiplier/starting-calories preview with low-calorie warning; collapsed **"Advanced"** disclosure (renamed from "Advanced settings", with a rotating chevron) hiding calculation mode, multiplier override, deficit/surplus incl. custom, phase interval, deficit taper toggle — collapsed by default so most users never see multiplier settings unless they intentionally expand it; Data section (export/import/reset). The initial onboarding screen (`renderSetupScreen`) labels this field "Current weight" (accurate at that moment — you're telling the app your weight right now). The in-app Settings tab (`renderSettings`), which edits the same `profile.startWeight` value later, labels it **"Starting weight"** instead with a helper note ("the baseline your roadmap was built from, not necessarily today's weight") — matching the terminology already used on the Calc Details page, since by the time someone revisits Settings weeks later their actual current weight (shown on the dashboard) has usually diverged from this fixed baseline.

## Dashboard hierarchy pass (UX polish, no logic/calculation changes)
A later pass reorganized the dashboard for daily-use ergonomics rather than changing any calorie/roadmap math:
- **Insights disclosure**: everything past the Quick log block (Weekly insight, Current strategy, Estimated timeline, Trail SVG, both weight charts, both calorie charts) is wrapped in a single `<details class="disclosure">` collapsed by default, so first paint only shows the four things a daily user actually needs (current weight, today's target, next milestone, what to log). Native `<details>` keeps the collapsed content in the DOM (not removed), so chart-rendering functions still run normally and controls bound inside them (e.g. the calorie-breakdown filter chips) work whether or not the section is expanded. Every filter-chip click still triggers a full `renderDashboard()` innerHTML rebuild, though, which previously reset `<details id="dash-insights">` back to collapsed on every click — `renderDashboard()` now reads its `.open` state before rebuilding and re-applies it on the fresh markup, so expanding Insights and then changing a filter no longer closes it back up.
- **Milestone card** (replaces the old inline "🏁 next phase" / "🎉 goal reached" banner text): shows the next phase's target weight, distance remaining ("Only 0.1 lb away" vs. "3.4 lbs remaining"), and a small progress bar scoped to the *current phase segment* (previous phase target → active phase target), not the whole journey. A `.reached` class swaps in a tinted pine background for the goal-reached state; left as a static style rather than a triggered animation (a `.celebrate`-class hook and comment mark where a future one-time celebration effect could be wired in, deliberately not auto-playing on every render since the dashboard re-renders on every tab visit).
- **Hero card**: "Today's target" calories bumped to the same visual weight as current weight (34px, gold accent) since it's the number a user acts on daily; current weight stays the identity anchor on the left.
- **`bindDisclosureAnimations()`**: a small shared helper (bound fresh each render, since `innerHTML` rebuilds the DOM every time) that fades in whichever element follows `<summary>` when any `<details>` is opened — used by the Insights disclosure, the nested Current-strategy disclosure, and the Log tab's entry-form disclosures, so expanding a section feels like a deliberate reveal rather than an instant jump.
- **Bottom nav**: the active tab now gets a bolder label, a slightly scaled icon, and a small dot indicator beneath it, on top of the existing pine color change — makes the active tab identifiable at a glance.
- Spacing/motion pass: consistent `.dash-top` vertical rhythm across the hero/milestone/stat-grid/quick-log block, plus small CSS transitions on cards, buttons (press scale), chips, phase rows, and log entries — no new libraries, existing color palette/typography/card language untouched throughout.

## Calorie breakdown chart (`renderCalorieBreakdownCard`, on the Trail page)
Hand-rolled SVG donut chart (`renderDonutChart` — manual `stroke-dasharray`/`stroke-dashoffset` circle segments, not the SVG2 `pathLength` shortcut, for broader browser compatibility) showing what share of logged calories came from each `MEAL_CATEGORIES` bucket, with the total in the center and a legend listing each category's calories + percentage.
- **Date-range filter**: a chip row (`DATE_FILTERS`) — Today / Yesterday / This Week / Last Week / This Month / Last Month / This Year / Last Year / All Time. `getDateRange(preset)` resolves a preset to concrete start/end `Date`s (weeks run Sunday–Saturday; "All Time" spans from the earliest weight-or-nutrition log date to today); `categoryTotalsForRange(preset)` sums `meals[].calories` across all `nutritionLogs` entries whose date falls in that range, bucketed by category (any non-standard label — from legacy data — folds into "Other").
- **Compare mode**: a "Compare" toggle chip reveals a second filter row. With it on, `renderCompareView` renders two smaller donuts side-by-side (no legend on the minis, just the center total) labeled by their respective range, followed by a per-category table showing `A → B` calories and the signed delta (colored pine for a decrease, danger-red for an increase), and an overall total delta line. Lets a user directly answer "did I eat more Dinner calories this week vs. last week."
- Filter/compare state (`calorieBreakdownFilter`, `calorieBreakdownCompareOn`, `calorieBreakdownCompareFilter`) lives in module-level variables, not persisted — resets to "This Week" / compare-off on reload. Selecting any chip re-renders the whole dashboard view (`renderDashboard()`), consistent with the rest of the app's full-re-render style.

## Clothing size estimate (`SIZE_TABLES`, `estimateSizeForWeight`) — gender-specific
Two static reference tables, selected by `profile.sex` (`SIZE_TABLES = {male, female}`, falls back to `male` if unset), built from Charles River Apparel's corporate size-equivalence chart (a Corporate Casuals spec-sheet PDF) plus the user's own data:
- **`MALE_SIZE_TABLE`** (13 rows, 190–310 lbs in 10lb steps: waist, pants, shirt) — unchanged from the original single-table version; this *is* the user's real personal measurement data, just relabeled as the male table. Shirt sizing follows the chart's Men's CRA tiers (S–5XL).
- **`FEMALE_SIZE_TABLE`** (30 rows, 115–260 lbs in 5lb steps) — the chart itself only defines 7 CRA size tiers (Women's Pants: waist/hip → CRA size, e.g. `M (10/12)`; Women's Shirt: chest → CRA size), whose waist/hip ranges and CRA labels are the chart's real measurement data at 7 weight anchors that are themselves *not* from the chart (which is measurement-based, not weight-based) — they're an approximation from standard women's size-to-weight correlation, called out with an extra disclaimer sentence wherever the table is shown. The top tier (3XL) has no pants entry in the source chart, so its waist/hip continue the same interpolation using the extrapolated 2XL/3XL-boundary values rather than new source data (hip ≈ waist + 11", a ratio that holds consistently across every PDF-sourced tier). The stored table breaks those 7 tiers down to 5lb increments (linearly interpolating waist/hip between the two nearest tiers, same math `estimateSizeForWeight` applies at render time — baked into the table so the Settings reference view itself shows finer rows) for more granular tracking; pants/shirt take whichever tier is nearest by weight, with an exact-midpoint tie (e.g. 140 lbs, exactly between the S and M tiers) resolved to the larger size.
- Field shape differs by gender to match how each chart actually defines sizing: male rows interpolate a numeric `pantsLow`/`pantsHigh` (real US pants sizing); female rows have an extra `hipLow`/`hipHigh` (interpolated) and a categorical `pants` CRA label (snapped to nearest row, like `shirt`) instead of a numeric pants size.

`estimateSizeForWeight(w, sex)` maps a weight to an estimate using the table for that sex. Waist (and, for female, hip) are numeric ranges, **linearly interpolated** between the two nearest rows (or **linearly extrapolated** past the table's ends using the slope of the nearest two rows); pants/shirt are categorical, snapping to the closest-by-weight row instead of interpolating. Surfaced in three places, each gender-appropriate and none showing the other gender's sizes/labels, with the same base disclaimer ("Estimated from a personal reference table (weight-to-size), not a substitute for actual measurements."):
- **Dashboard** (`renderSizeEstimateCard`, inside the Insights disclosure, right after the Journey trail card): current rolling-average weight vs. goal weight, side by side. Adds a Hip row for female profiles.
- **Roadmap tab**: each phase row gets an extra `~waist X" · shirt` line under its calorie targets (same two fields for both genders, kept compact), so sizing progress is visible phase by phase alongside the existing weight/calorie roadmap.
- **Settings** ("Clothing size reference"): a plain reference table for manual lookup, placed between the Food search and Data sections. Columns adapt to sex (female adds a Hip column; Pants/Shirt show CRA labels instead of numeric pants sizes); female's table also appends the weight-anchor caveat above to the disclaimer line.

## Calculation details page (`openCalcDetails`)
A full-screen overlay (not a tab) reachable from the dashboard's strategy card. Walks through every step with real numbers plugged in: inputs → BMR formula → TDEE → calculated multiplier → starting calories (Day 1, fixed) → metabolic calibration (if active) → current phase (BMR recalculated at that weight → TDEE → tapered eating target). Falls back to a simpler weight×multiplier breakdown for legacy profiles.

## Notable implementation details
- Pure vanilla JS, template-string based re-rendering (`innerHTML` swaps), no framework, no libraries.
- `leastSquaresSlope` is a shared regression helper used by the trend chart, the recent-window completion estimate, and the calibration engine.
- `todayStr()` builds the date from local `Date` getters (not `toISOString()`) — the original UTC-based version could disagree with the locally-rendered header date in the evening for US/Americas users, causing "today" lookups (e.g. "eaten today") to mismatch.
- Trail SVG hover tooltips are custom (not native `<title>`): a transparent oversized hit-circle per point drives `mousemove`/`mouseleave` handlers positioning a floating tooltip `div`, clamped to stay inside the scrollable container.
- No image/chart libraries — all visualizations are hand-rolled inline SVG, including the calorie-breakdown donut.
- `MEAL_CATEGORY_COLORS` maps each of the 6 meal categories to an existing CSS custom property (gold/pine/pine-dark/moss) plus two additions introduced for this chart: `--sky` (Drinks) and reusing `--ink-soft` (Other) — kept in the same muted, low-saturation palette as the rest of the UI rather than introducing a separate "chart color" system.
- **Calorie line-item rows** (in the activity form and its edit row) are kept in an in-memory draft array (`activityDraftMeals` / `editActivityDraftMeals`) rather than re-read from the DOM on save. Typing in a row's label/calories only mutates that array entry and updates the total text directly (`oninput`) — it does *not* re-render the row markup, which would drop input focus on every keystroke. Only explicit Add/Remove clicks re-render the row list.

## Known limitations
- Single-device only (localStorage) — export/import is the only backup path; no automatic reminders to back up.
- No unit toggle — lbs/inches only.
- No authentication/multi-profile support; one profile per browser origin.
- Activity level is fixed at setup and doesn't vary day-to-day (no exercise-calorie logging) — calibration partially compensates over time, but there's no way to add back a specific workout's calories.
- Deficit/surplus math and calibration use the standard 3,500 kcal/lb energy-density conversion — a well-established approximation, not exact for any individual.
- No food database or barcode scanning — calorie/macro entry is manual.
- `WeightTrendEngine`'s full-history `overall` trend (used only as the forecast's conservative clamp, not as the "current trend" headline) is intentionally **all-time**, not scoped to the current profile/goal — a user who changed goals or regimes mid-history can see the forecast report `'conflicting'` rather than a falsely precise date. Same v1 boundary `estimateCompletion()`'s `trackedDays` gate already accepted; scoping to `profile.createdDate` was considered and deferred (that field isn't reliably set on older profiles, and no other trend/calibration code in the app scopes by it).
- A goal-change milestone's `reachedDate` is the date the goal was edited in Settings, not necessarily the exact date the old goal was first crossed (no historical point-in-time rolling average exists to compute that) — not shown prominently in the UI for this reason.
